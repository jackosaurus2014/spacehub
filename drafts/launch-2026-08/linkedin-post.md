SpaceNexus started as a place to follow the space industry — launches, markets, jobs, funding
rounds. Today we're launching something we've been building alongside all of that: a game.

**Space Tycoon** is a free browser (and mobile) MMO where players run space corporations across
a realistically modeled 2150 solar system. It came from a simple observation: the most
interesting thing happening in space right now isn't science fiction — it's business. Launch
economics, orbital real estate, supply chains, labor markets, capital. So we built a strategy
game about exactly that.

Three decisions shaped it:

**No combat.** Every space strategy game defaults to fleet battles. Ours has none — all
competition is economic. Players win by out-producing, out-bidding, out-researching, and
out-maneuvering: price wars in shared demand pools, market cornering on a live order book,
talent poaching with counteroffer windows, and a hostile-takeover system with tender offers and
minority-shareholder protections. It's corporate strategy as gameplay.

**Real data.** SpaceNexus already ingests NOAA space-weather feeds and the global launch
manifest for our news and tracking tools — so the game reads the same data. A real geomagnetic
storm surfaces as an in-game storm watch. A real rocket an hour from liftoff opens an in-game
bonus window. Real Starship and Artemis milestones fire research bonuses. We think this is a
genuinely new kind of connection between a game and the industry it's modeled on.

**A real economy.** One live price per commodity. Finite local demand, so competitors take each
other's customers. Buildings that consume inputs, deposits that deplete, wages that move. We
balance-test it the way you'd stress-test a financial model — a simulation harness runs
strategies against the actual game engine before we ship tuning changes.

And a commitment we've published as policy: **no pay-to-win, ever.** Real money can never buy
competitive advantage in Space Tycoon — cosmetics and convenience only. The game is free, runs
in any browser, and installs nothing.

It launched this week. The economy is live and the first corporations are forming.

If you work in the space industry, we'd love your eyes on it — you'll recognize more of your
day job in it than you might expect.
