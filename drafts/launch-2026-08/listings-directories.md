**Tags:** space, economy, tycoon, mmo, multiplayer, browser, management, trading, sci-fi, no-combat
**Play link:** https://spacenexus.us/space-tycoon
**Cover:** public/art/hero-space-tycoon.png (630×500 crop) · Screenshots: eight region banners

**Description:**
Space Tycoon is a browser-based space MMO where the market is the game.
Every player trades on one shared live economy: supply and demand set every
price, logistics cost money, orbital slots and belt corridors are finite, and
hardware — beams, satellite buses, life-support, fusion cores — has to be
manufactured at fabrication facilities before anyone can sell it.

There is no combat. Rivalry is economic: out-produce, out-bid, out-research,
corner a market, take a rival corporation over. Risk is real — solar storms,
equipment failure, NPC piracy, regulators — so insurance and redundancy are
strategy, not flavour.

- One live market for every commodity and service, with an order book
- Manufacturing chains from ore to station modules; five NPC industrial corps you compete with
- Corporations, alliances, mergers and hostile takeovers as the end-game
- A 270+ technology research tree across a real-solar-system map, Earth to the heliopause
- Real launch schedules and NOAA space weather feed the simulation
- Free. Cosmetic purchases only. No pay-to-win, by written policy.
- Fresh-world epochs so newcomers start on a level solar system

Made by SpaceNexus, a space-industry site that tracks every launch.

## Reddit — two more subreddits (post one per week; do not cross-post the same day)

### r/tycoon
**Title:** I built a browser tycoon MMO where the whole economy is one shared market and hardware has to be manufactured before anyone can sell it
**Body:**
Space Tycoon is free, runs in a browser tab, and has no combat — rivalry is economic. What makes it a tycoon game rather than a 4X: every price is supply and demand on one live market shared by every player; logistics (delta-v, storage, boil-off) cost real money; orbital slots and belt corridors are finite; profit and loss are visible per building and per location.

The bit I'm proudest of shipped this week: components and hardware left the NPC price curve entirely. Beams, satellite buses, life-support packs are made at fabrication facilities from mined inputs, and they only appear on the market when a player or one of five named NPC industrial corporations lists what they built. The NPC corps buy raw inputs at live prices (so they move iron and aluminum), manufacture, list at cost-plus, and bid for what they're short of — which means a player who fabricates electronics has a buyer waiting.

It's young (Epoch 2 started Aug 24, fresh world) and the player base is small, so this is a good moment to get in. No pay-to-win by written policy — cosmetics only.

Happy to answer economy-design questions.
