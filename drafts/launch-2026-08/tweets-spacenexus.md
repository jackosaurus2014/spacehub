## Game dev-log (these perform well with sim/strategy audiences; post any time)

**G1 — the 50-year playtest**
Before relaunching our game economy, we simulated 50 in-game years with 8 competing AI
corporations — including late joiners entering at year 10 and year 30. The year-10 joiner
went bankrupt and stayed bankrupt. Forever. So we fixed the "graduation cliff" before any
real player hits it.

**G2 — the mothball trap**
Our balance sim caught our own advice being wrong: we told players to mothball buildings
during a price war. Simulated it — for small corporations, mothballing is a trap (−19% net
worth vs just riding it out). The in-game guidance now says so. Simulate your game design,
folks.

**G3 — economic warfare pricing**
Design problem: a price-crash campaign cost $250M flat. Too expensive for small players
(never rational), pocket change for whales. Fix: fees now scale with the size of the market
you're attacking — 15% of a week's turnover. Attack a small market cheaply, pay real money
to crash a big one.

**G4 — no combat, on purpose**
Space Tycoon has no fleets and no lasers. You fight with prices, supply chains, poached
engineers, and cornered commodities. The sim says tit-for-tat retaliation bankrupts both
sides while bystanders profit — which is exactly the deterrence we wanted.
