## Notes, not for posting

**The news, 2026-09-16.** Avio's CEO said commercial satellite operators
turned away by SpaceX are approaching Avio, following SpaceX's suspension of
new commercial Falcon bookings beyond 2028. Avio is expanding production at
Colleferro; Vega C is configured for up to four flights a year and Avio has
said it may need to move toward six. Reported by European Spaceflight and
SatNews today; the underlying SpaceX decision was broken by Bloomberg in July.

- https://europeanspaceflight.com/avio-ceo-reports-influx-of-customers-turned-away-by-spacex
- http://satnews.com/2026/09/16/avio-reports-influx-of-commercial-satellite-customers-as-spacex-freezes-new-falcon-9-orders/

**Our number.** Falcon 9 at 109 launches in 2026 to date is from the
SpaceNexus Launch Cadence Index, read 2026-09-15. An attempt is a launch that
lifted off. Re-check it before posting if a day or two has passed — it only
moves up.

**Why this one.** It is the first concrete evidence of the demand shift the
LinkedIn post argued about, it arrived today, and it is the same company whose
Vega-C launch prompted the telemetry fix two nights ago. The 4-versus-109
comparison is the whole argument in one line, and both figures are real.

**Deliberately not claimed:** that Avio can't or won't scale, or that Europe
is failing. The post says the demand moved and the capacity hasn't yet, which
is what the numbers support and nothing more. Avio going from four flights to
six is a 50% increase and genuinely hard work; it is simply small against 109.
