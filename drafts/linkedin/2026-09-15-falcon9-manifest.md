SpaceX now flies just under half of everything humanity puts into orbit. This year it has 109 of the world's 226 orbital launch attempts.

In July it stopped taking new commercial Falcon bookings beyond 2028 — including Transporter, the rideshare programme a large part of the smallsat industry quietly built its business model on.

Those two facts belong in the same sentence more often than they appear in one.

The reflex answer is that the market will provide. Maybe it will. But it is worth attaching numbers to the assumption.

Year to date, SpaceX has flown 109 times. Rocket Lab, ULA and Arianespace have flown 25 times between them. That is not a criticism of any of them — Rocket Lab is up four on last year and flying beautifully — it is the arithmetic of what exists today against what would need to exist.

And the global picture is flat: 226 orbital attempts this year against 224 by the same date in 2025. Under one percent. The industry is not currently growing its way out of this.

Three honest caveats. SpaceX being down eleven year-on-year looks like deliberate reallocation toward Starlink and Starship, not weakness. Starship is the intended answer and appears in none of these numbers yet. And Chinese cadence is climbing, from 55 attempts to 64.

bookings for missions beyond 2028, including the Transporter sun-synchronous
rideshares and the mid-inclination Bandwagon missions. First reported by
