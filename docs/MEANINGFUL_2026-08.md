# Space Tycoon — Meaningful Decisions, Saturation Curves & Economic Offense

**Spec date:** 2026-08-15. Companion to CLAUDE.md §Space Tycoon,
[ECONOMY_PVP_2026-08.md](ECONOMY_PVP_2026-08.md) (waves E1–E7, shipped),
[BALANCE.md](BALANCE.md), [SESSION_DESIGN.md](SESSION_DESIGN.md),
[STATS_DESIGN.md](STATS_DESIGN.md), [POLICY.md](POLICY.md), [LORE.md](LORE.md).

**Founder directive (verbatim):** *"make sure every decision in our game is
meaningful. every decision should have an impact in terms of building or
researching. players shouldn't just be able to keep buying endless satellites
in orbit or data centers to keep making money. there need to be diminishing
returns due to saturation of markets and the game needs to factor costs into
the game to try and offset incomes so player incomes don't scale out of
control. there also should be meaningful ways for players to cut into other
company's profits and projects in a true economic PVP game. design this all
out as a live service, long term PVP economic live service MMO space strategy
game that is one of a kind."*

**Method:** this spec does not assert — it simulates. `scripts/sim-harness.ts`
(kept — a reusable tool) imports the **actual engine modules** — the demand-pool
aggregator and pool-share pricing (`demand-pools.ts` + `service-pricing.ts`),
the E3 consumption engine (`consumption.ts`, called directly), extraction
pressure (`extraction-pressure.ts`), the scaling-sink formulas
(`formulas.ts`), the labor market (`labor-market.ts`), and the real
building/service/resource definitions — and runs scripted strategies over 24
game-months, solo and with 2–3 competitors sharing one pool. Every table below
is regenerated by `npx tsx scripts/sim-strategies.ts` (deterministic; no DB).
When a constant changes, re-run and the tables update.

Fidelity notes (stated so nobody over-reads the numbers): buildings complete
instantly (real timers are minutes vs 6-real-hour game months); the ~14
private multipliers (research/commanders/legacy/tier/…) are held at **1.0** to
isolate the structural curves, with a ×2.0 sensitivity table included;
market-policy inputs are bought at basePrice × 1.08 (NPC maker minimum
half-spread 6% + 2% fee — the real floor cost of the `market` supply policy);
mined output sells at basePrice × 0.97; season modifier neutral; `active30d = 0`
(full NPC demand floor — the *most generous* pool setting).

## 1. Part 1 — Simulation results: does the shipped economy meet the directive?

### 1.0 Verdict up front

**The founder's core requirement is now structurally MET** — you cannot keep
buying satellites or datacenters to keep making money; past a small N, each
additional copy *loses* money, and because operating costs are **not** scaled
down by the demand pool while revenue is, over-building is actively punished,
not just diminished. Rival entry genuinely cuts an incumbent's revenue
(−33% in the GEO scenario below), and a shared deposit genuinely thins
(each of 3 co-located miners settles at ~2/3 of solo output).

**But the tuning around that correct skeleton is broken in five places:** the
tier ladder is economically inverted (T4/T5 flagships are negative-ROI on the
*first* copy), one authored demand floor contradicts its own authoring rule
(Earth launch), mining's cash revenue is still market-blind, the net-worth
metric that drives the wealth tax / Frontier / leagues ignores assets
entirely, and there is **no decommission mechanic** — an over-built player
has no exit decision and bleeds forever. Waves M1–M6 (§5) fix these, tuning
first.

### 1.5 Findings register (F1–F10, each quantified)

**PASSES — the E1–E7 skeleton achieves the directive:**

- **P1. Saturation is real and sharp.** Marginal satellite ROI: 13.5%/mo (N=1)
  → 0 (N=5) → negative forever (N≥6). Even ×2.0 multipliers only move the
  crossing to N≈7. *"Keep buying satellites to keep making money" is dead.*
- **P2. Costs bend income — hard.** Operating/maintenance/overhead/input costs
  are **not** discounted by the pool multiplier while revenue is, so a
  saturated fleet's income isn't merely flat — it's negative ($-23.9M/mo at
  21 sats). Income cannot "scale out of control": the 21-building spammer's
  *total* revenue ($26M) is beneath a 10-building integrator's ($61M).
- **P3. Rivals take your customers and thin your seams.** −33% incumbent
  revenue on entrant arrival; 65–67% per-miner output on a shared deposit.
- **P4. Vertical integration and diversification are the dominant *earned*
  strategy** — the integrator is the only profile with durable positive,
  growing income. That's the "meaningful decisions" outcome the directive
  wants.

**FAILURES — where the curves break the founder's intent:**

- **F1. The tier ladder is economically inverted.** First-copy ROI by tier
  (build-menu sweep, §2.1): T1 satellite 13.5%/mo (7-month payback) →
  T2 datacenter 2.0%/mo → T3 mining 0.28%/mo (354 months) → T4/T5 **negative
  or 500–5,700-month payback**; `fabrication_titan`, `datacenter_jupiter`,
  `mining_kuiper`, `sat_mars_relay` lose money on their FIRST copy. The
  rational player never leaves LEO; the aspirational deep-space content is a
  wealth-destruction trap. Progression must pay a frontier premium, not a
  frontier penalty. *(Root causes: T4/T5 maintenance authored at 25–60× T1
  levels while their pools/service grosses are only ~10× — and remote
  locations' pool floors don't scale with capex.)*
- **F2. Two authored demand floors violate the authoring rule** ("floor ≈
  2.5–3.5× the flagship service revenue"): `earth_surface.launch` is $60M vs
  a $55M flagship capacity (**1.09×**) — the Heavy Launch Pad is saturated at
  N=1 (0.708 pool, $269K/mo net on an $800M building, 2,979-month payback,
  while its tooltip still promises "$35M/mo net, ~23-month payback").
  `mars_orbit.launch` ($15M) vs `svc_propellant_brokerage` ($40M capacity):
  **0.375×** — `sat_mars_relay`'s first copy lands at the 0.35 floor,
  −$3.1M/mo. Systematically: with `NPC_SUPPLY_FRACTION = 0.5` eating half of
  every pool, effective player headroom is (D − 0.5·D_floor), so several
  "3× flagship" floors actually carry <2 player copies (LEO compute: 1.7).
- **F3. Mining's cash revenue is market-blind.** `mining_output` services are
  pool-exempt (correct per spec — they were to compete "through the shared
  market price"), but the engine still pays their flat `revenuePerMonth`
  ($50M asteroid, $160M Titan) with no spot-price linkage; extraction
  pressure brakes only the *units* (~15% of a rig's income). Result: the one
  vertical with no shared-market discipline on its dominant cash flow, and
  the "crash the iron market" play still can't touch a rival miner's service
  income. The three-price-surfaces defect (ECONOMY_PVP §1c) survives here.
- **F4. Net worth ignores assets — and the wealth tax hits the wrong people.**
  `computeNetWorth = money + totalEarned − totalSpent` (frontier.ts:28, also
  inlined in game-engine.ts §1c) counts capex as destroyed wealth and double-
  counts operating flows. Sim: the 21-satellite spammer's "net worth" reads
  **−$2.54B** (exec comp: $0/mo) while the 3-building idler reads +$1.9B and
  pays $537K/mo. Executive compensation — BALANCE.md's wealth-scaling sink —
  **exempts builders and taxes savers**, exactly backwards. The same metric
  drives Frontier graduation, espionage target brackets, and league
  seeding, so asset-heavy corps are systematically under-bracketed.
- **F5. No decommission/mothball mechanic exists** (zero grep hits for
  demolish/decommission/scrap-as-mechanic). Over-building is punished (P2)
  but irrecoverable — a player who mis-expands bleeds maintenance forever
  with no exit decision. A punishing economy without an exit is a death
  spiral, not a lesson; churn risk for exactly the players who engaged most.
- **F6. Derived demand is too weak to replace the receding NPC floor.** A LEO
  telecom sat adds $0.2M/mo of derived telecom demand but $3.5M of capacity
  (5.7%); a datacenter adds $0.25M compute demand vs $12M capacity (2.1%).
  `npcPopulationScaler` shrinks D_npc to 40% at 500 actives and 10% at 5,000
  — but player growth adds capacity ~17× faster than it adds demand, so the
  server-wide equilibrium slides toward the 0.35 floor as the game *succeeds*.
  The macro curve punishes population growth; at 10k MAU the whole service
  economy converges on saturation. Demand must grow with the economy (M3).
- **F7. The 0.35 floor breaks the pool cap at extreme saturation.** Below
  D/C = 0.35 the floor pays 0.35 × C total, which exceeds D — "total
  extraction never exceeds the pool" fails exactly when spam is heaviest.
  Costs keep it unprofitable (P2), so this is integrity-theoretical — but
  it's the invariant BALANCE.md cites, so fix or re-document.
- **F8. The market-event schedule is a client-computable oracle.**
  `getGlobalActiveMarketEvents` is deterministically seeded from world time —
  by design (shared world). But that means anyone reading the code can
  compute *future* events and pre-position risk-free (helium3 ×2.0 → $41.9M
  bounded print per event, ~16% round-trip cost vs an 84–100% move). Either
  publish the forecast to **everyone** in-game (make it fair intel — the
  better fit for "data access is gameplay") or move the roll server-side.
- **F9. Build-menu tooltips lie post-E3/E4.** Authored payback claims
  ("$35M/mo net", "payback ~23 months", "37 months") predate demand pools and
  input consumption; §2.1's table shows real first-copy paybacks of 50–2,979
  months. Every tooltip's economics paragraph must be regenerated from the
  live formulas (or replaced by a live P&L preview — better).
- **F10. Trading beats building at the ceiling, risk-free** (see 1.1d):
  deterministic events + NPC maker liquidity put the attentive trader's
  bound above the best builder's income, with zero capex at risk. Volume
  caps save it from being unbounded; F8's fix plus event-window maker-spread
  widening (M4) brings it into line.

### 2.2 The other decision surfaces

| Surface | Verdict | Evidence / gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Research picks** | ⚠ Mostly free wins with opportunity cost only | ~290 techs; effects are additive bonuses. ### 3.2 Designed additions (every tool: cost, counterplay, Frontier shield, no combat)

**O1. Hostile takeovers & M&A — the canon end-game (wave M6).**
Corporations gain a **share registry**: 100 shares, founder starts with 100.
Shares become acquirable through (a) voluntary sales, (b) **capital raises**
the board votes on (sell 10–30% for cash — a real early-game financing
decision), and (c) **distress auctions**: a corporation in cash-negative
territory ≥ 3 consecutive game-months has 5–15% tranches auto-auctioned
(replaces today's silent bankruptcy-protection with a stakes-bearing event —
and finally gives F5's over-builders an exit that transfers assets instead of
deleting them).
- **Tender offer:** any corp/alliance may bid for outstanding shares at a
  named price, open 7 real days (weekly loop). Target board may counteroffer
  (buyback at ≥ bid, burning cash) or solicit a white knight.
- **Due diligence is purchased intel:** bidders buy a diligence report
  (espionage-priced, imperfect — ±15% noise on cash/P&L) before bidding;
  never free, never perfect (canon).
- **Minority protections (canon):** any holder crossing 50% must offer
  minorities the same per-share price (mandatory bid rule); minorities may
  instead hold and collect **dividends** (new: boards set a payout ratio —
  itself a governance decision).
- **What control gets you:** consolidated P&L, the target continues as a
  **subsidiary** (existing subsidiaries system becomes real M&A inventory),
  its pool capacity/share counts toward yours, its era/doctrine locked to
  parent's next charter.
- **Costs/risks:** premium over "fair value" (new book-value metric from M1
  — F4's fix is a *prerequisite* for fair M&A), arbitration fees, integration
  malus (−10% target revenue for 2 game-months). **Frontier corps cannot be
  tendered, ever** (canon on-ramp); voluntary sale requires graduation.
- **No combat:** control changes by ledgered share transactions only.

**O2. Targeted price wars / dumping — with real ammunition (M5, needs M3's
price-linked mining).** Once mining/service cash flows read live spot (M3),
selling inventory below spot genuinely compresses every producer's margin at
that (location, resource). Design: a corporation may declare a **price
campaign** on one market (public, on the diplomacy feed — reputation is
legible): its asks are exempt from the position-guard spread-widening for 7
days, letting it push price toward the 0.3× band floor. Cost: it eats the
margin sacrifice on real volume + a campaign fee (burned). Counterplay:
rivals **buy the dump** (cheap inventory), hedge with forwards (E8/M5), or
out-wait — bands + Earth-import parity + NPC maker caps bound the damage;
mean-reversion (~6.6-cycle half-life) makes campaigns expensive to sustain.
Frontier players' input purchases are unaffected (they buy at the NPC maker,
which never quotes below band).

**O3. Input denial / cornering — formalized, bounded (M5).** Cornering is
already legitimate-but-expensive (position guard at 40% of 7-day volume,
import parity ceiling). Add the missing *offensive read*: `market_microstructure`
tech (E-wave) holders see **aggregate rival standing-order demand** per
resource — so a corner can be aimed at what rivals actually need (satellite
buses vs a datacenter corp; life-support vs a station corp). Counterplay:
victims switch supply policy to local production (the E3 toggle — this is
what makes it a *decision*), buy through Earth import (×2.5 premium — pain,
not death), or sign supply forwards. Denial premium decays via NPC restock.
Frontier: NPC maker guarantees Frontier players' recipe inputs at band.

**O4. Talent poaching — aimable wage war (M5).** New espionage-tier action
`poach_talent(target, crewType, n)` : offer signing bonuses (6-month salary ×
wage index × 1.5, escrowed) to raid up to n ≤ 10% of a rival's named crew
type. Target gets a 48h **counteroffer window** (match 75% of the bonus to
retain — a real, priced decision on both sides). Success also bumps the
*global* wage index contribution for that type (poaching wars heat the whole
market — self-limiting). Costs: bonuses + espionage action fee + reputation
hit if detected (security crew reduce detection… finally a reason to hire
`security`). Shields: Frontier corps immune both directions; per-target
cooldown 30 days; `guild_arbitration` tech grants one free retention/season.

**O5. Predatory slot leasing — allowed, taxed (M5 guardrail).** Leasing slots
you don't build on is legitimate denial (real carrying cost: burned bid).
Guardrails so it can't be total lockout: unbuilt leases pay an **escalating
idle fee** (10% of winning bid per 30 days, burned) and auto-release at 90
days unbuilt; lease transfer market lets victims buy their way in at
market-clearing prices (canon: "ownership transfers at market-clearing
prices").

**O6. Chokepoint squeeze — lane concessions (M5/M6).** The zone governor may
levy a **freight toll** (0.5–2%) on rival dispatches crossing their zone's
lanes (lane metadata exists in `spatial-strategy.ts`; the governor-tax pipe
exists in `sync` §6d — this is one more consumer). Tolls are public
(diplomacy feed), capped, and Frontier-exempt. Counterplay: alternate routes
cost real Δv (geography as strategy), or vote the governor out (zone
influence is contestable), or negotiate a treaty (alliance diplomacy's
`tradeBonus` already reduces fees — treaties finally have a stick to match
the carrot).

**O7. Contract sniping — keep it, add the counter-decision (M5, small).**
Sealed-bid already prevents literal last-second reads; add **bid insurance**
(pay 5% of collateral to learn, post-award, the margin you lost by — paid
intel that improves next bids) and per-contract **soft-close** (bids in the
final 10 minutes extend the window 10 minutes) for the open-outcry slot
auctions so sniping there is a strategy with counterplay rather than a
timing gimmick.

**O8. Espionage stays info-only — but the info gets sharper (M5).** No
target-side harm (POLICY.md line). New purchasable products: rival demand-pool
share trend (their `prevPlayerShare` deltas), input-dependency report (what
they consume monthly — the O3 targeting data), labor roster summary (the O4
targeting data). Each priced, cooldown-gated, and visible-to-victim as a
counterintelligence event 25% of the time (risk).

Every O-tool passes the checklist: real money/reputation cost, a defender
decision that beats the attack at some price, Frontier immunity, no kinetic
loss, deterministic-client boundary respected (all shared state server-side,
delivered via existing sync snapshot pipes).

## 4. Part 4 — What makes this one-of-a-kind (live-service positioning)

The unique combination no competitor has: **(1) pure-economic PvP with a
hard no-combat covenant** (EVE's economy with the gank layer deleted — the
whole risk surface is hazards, markets, and contracts, so "loss" never means
"logged in to find my ship gone to a player"); **(2) a deterministic client
sim + shared server markets** (offline-tolerant, mobile-first, replayable,
save-scum-proof — OGame's accessibility with an actual economy); **(3)
real-world grounding** — live launch data, real orbital mechanics flavor,
NASA textures, a 22nd-century canon timeline, hosted inside a real space-
industry portal whose users *are* the target audience; **(4) radical market
transparency as gameplay** — price history, flow maps, demand pools, public
quarterlies; the skill expression is analysis, not APM.

**Three signature mechanics to lean into (marketing-visible, each already
half-built):**

1. **The Public Quarterly** (E6) — every corporation's auto-published
   report, rivalry-feed rankings, "earnings season" as a monthly-loop event.
   No other MMO makes corporate reporting the drama engine. Cheap to
   amplify: earnings-week league metric, analyst-note UGC, era medals tied
   to report streaks.
2. **Visible demand, contested pools** (E4) — the demand map is the game's
   "star map". Ship the Market Intelligence heat view as the flagship screen:
   where is undersupplied, who's crowding whom, where the next $100M/mo of
   demand is. This is the strategy layer EVE hides in spreadsheets, made
   first-class.
3. **The Forecastable World** — super-cycles announce 7 days ahead; NPC
   procurement drives publish ahead; hazard forecasts exist; F8's fix turns
   market events into public forecasts too. A strategy game about *positioning
   for announced futures* rather than reacting — unique, and perfectly suited
   to a daily-check-in mobile cadence.
